Distinguish these two kinds of change:

MINOR SURFACE VARIATION: fine grain, mild mottling, small tonal shifts, and faint
translucent patterns that leave the underlying skin shading, photographic detail,
and facial geometry intact. Such variation may be identifiable in comparison,
extend across several facial areas, or resemble shapes or lettering. It remains
minor when it is low-contrast and does not dominate ordinary whole-image viewing.
An impression of changed expression or facial contour caused by faint shading
is not, by itself, evidence of geometric distortion or overall damage.

CONSPICUOUS CONTAMINATION: broad, visibly artificial texture on an originally
plain surface, such as a backdrop acquiring a clearly visible field of repeated
tiles, maze patterns, stripes, seams, or cloudy blotches. This can degrade the
whole photograph even when transparent and even when the person remains intact.
Evaluate its repetition, visible contrast, spatial scale, and extent together.
Apply this criterion to every surface; skin and clothing are not exempt.

PASS when changes are limited to minor surface variation and the photograph
retains a natural, coherent overall appearance. FAIL for conspicuous
contamination, dominant broad discoloration, strong obstruction, or actual
visible displacement/distortion of facial features. For a structural rejection,
identify which feature's geometry is visibly distorted; a shading or texture
difference alone is insufficient. Likewise, a detected faint pattern alone is
insufficient for a contamination rejection: explain its conspicuous impact on
the complete photograph at the supplied viewing size.

Rate none when no added change is apparent; minor for the surface variations
above; moderate for clear overall degradation; severe for extensive prominent
damage. Report overall_assessment first. PASS requires damage_type none or
surface_variation and only none/minor artifacts. FAIL requires another
damage_type with a moderate/severe artifact. Keep these fields consistent.
